# Experimental rear-quarter camera

`chase_preview.py` renders a short, local third-person shot around one flight instant. It is an alternate camera study, separate from the north-up globe film. It does not automatically edit the shot into a trip or replace an approved export.

```sh
python3 "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/chase_preview.py" trip.json \
  --assets data/bundle --flight 1 --at 2029-11-03T17:00:00+00:00 \
  --duration 6 --time-scale 6 --width 360 --fps 24 --samples 8 \
  --device auto --workdir renders/chase --frames 0,143
```

Replace the example timestamp with an airborne instant inside the selected flight. The complete physical interval must fit within that leg. Inspect the spot checks, then omit `--frames` to render and verify the full silent H.264 clip. `--prepare-only` bakes the local assets and frame poses without starting Blender. `--blender` supplies an explicit executable. All inputs are local; preparation makes no downloads. Shared air and distant clouds are substantially more expensive than the earlier sky-only scene. The defaults are a 360×640 preview with eight samples; four samples can be useful for a faster motion study, while 32 or more are useful for reference stills. Measure selected frames before a full movie. At 360×640, one M1 Max dawn study took about 29–39 seconds per frame at eight samples and 132–156 seconds at 32; other scenes and machines vary. A full run has a four-hour timeout.

The camera sits behind and to port, looking toward the aircraft center with a level horizon. A modeled coordinated turn rolls smoothly from `--bank` degrees to level. Heading and position use physical time and stateless sampling, so frame order and output FPS do not change the maneuver. `--time-scale 6` compresses 36 physical seconds into a six-second video. Speed is an explicitly modeled 230 m/s. The selected itinerary supplies the initial position, heading, altitude and aircraft type; it does not supply the ensuing maneuver.

`--distance-lengths` controls the follow distance in physical aircraft lengths; `--fov` controls horizontal field of view. Widths must be multiples of 18 between 180 and 1080 for exact, even 9:16 output. Video duration is bounded to 1–12 seconds, physical duration to 120 seconds, bank to ±30°, and starting altitude to 1,500–15,000 m. A321neo, 777-200ER, 787-9 and 757-300 procedural illustrations are supported. This experiment does not load optional GLB replacements.

## Shared geometry and lighting

Aircraft, curved terrain, Sun and reconstructed cloud extinction share one metre-scale east/north/up frame and one perspective camera in Blender Cycles. Clouds therefore attenuate camera and shadow rays through the same volume as the aircraft. This scene bypasses the overhead aircraft sprite/depth compositor. It uses the bundle's terrain calibration and procedural aircraft materials.

The cloud bakes sample the existing dated reconstruction, convert its exaggerated radial heights back to physical metres, and retain extinction in inverse physical metres. Float atlases and trilinear shaders interpolate the field. Weather is frozen at one shared midpoint instant, including explicit replay mapping when configured.

`--cloudscape layered` is the default. It combines an 80 km-wide near field and a 1,000 km-wide distant field, each sampled at 64×160×160 nodes. Both use radial MSL height from 120 to 16,000 m, so distant layers follow Earth's curvature. The paired renderer records carry one shared definition of layer extents and fades. Complementary weights are evaluated analytically after native density sampling, transitioning from near to far across 28–40 km from the origin; the distant field fades across 450–500 km. These are finite rendering boundaries, not meteorological edges. `--cloudscape local` retains the original 80 km tangent-coordinate bake, with its outer 12-percent fade and no distant field.

`--cloud-shape` (0–1, default 1) applies only to layered clouds. It uses Earth-fixed, deterministic vertical warping to move existing cloud support by at most 1,000 m at full strength. The warp vanishes at the ground and above 12 km; it preserves the source column's geography, so a completely clear weather column remains empty. Its density Jacobian preserves radial column optical depth analytically, with error from finite atlas sampling. It does not preserve every oblique path's optical depth or provide observed billows.

`--cloud-detail` (0–1, default 1) adds stationary 750 m procedural erosion for the close viewpoint. At full strength it multiplies local density by 0.05–1.95. Empty macro cells remain empty, but this extra modulation does not conserve column optical depth. Use `--cloud-shape 0 --cloud-detail 0` to inspect the dated reconstruction without these extra silhouette and erosion treatments.

For layered clouds, Geometry Nodes evaluates the coarse curved atlas and its support bounds into native density grids before Cycles samples it. Paired blend/fade weights and nearby 750 m erosion remain analytic material terms evaluated after each native density lookup; they are not resampled into the mismatched near/far grids. The near grid is 160×160×64 and the far grid is 160×160×192 (about 1.64 and 4.92 million nodes); the helper rejects more than eight million nodes in a grid. Grid coordinates are physical world metres with an identity object transform, and the native `density` attribute stores extinction multiplied by 1,000,000; the scattering material divides by the same value to recover inverse-metre extinction. This keeps Blender’s default 0.001 native-volume clipping threshold near 10⁻⁹ per physical metre. Native grid precision can still quantize the stored density. This adds a second spatial resampling: curved source-height slices become a Cartesian grid with trilinear interpolation. Small gaps and sharp edges in the coarse density can soften, and near/far base estimates still differ with resolution. The analytic weights remain complementary; they do not make those two reconstructed density fields identical. Column and visual checks remain necessary. The legacy local mode retains direct procedural atlas evaluation. Native grids remove atlas image-tile evaluation from each volume event while retaining analytic ramps and erosion; actual render speed depends on the scene and machine and must be measured.

`--atmosphere volume` is the default. A spherical RGB atmosphere places Rayleigh scattering and aerosol scattering/absorption between camera, terrain, clouds, and aircraft. The one dated Sun enters at white scene-relative intensity; Cycles traces its attenuation through the same air used by view rays. A black world adds no independent sky illumination. The molecular and aerosol scale heights are 8,000 m and 1,200 m, and the atmosphere ends at 100 km. This mode requires Blender 5.0 or newer for Cycles null-scattering integration. Molecular scattering uses the Rayleigh phase function when available; an unavailable phase property falls back to isotropic scattering. Aerosols use a Henyey–Greenstein approximation with anisotropy 0.76. The scene allows six volume-scattering bounces. There is no ozone absorption or spectral transport; a buried coarse planet supplies distant occlusion outside the geographic terrain patch.

`--atmosphere sky` retains the earlier, faster comparison treatment: a small spherical calculation attenuates direct sunlight and Blender's Nishita sky supplies background and ambient light. That mode has no shared camera-to-terrain aerial perspective. Use `--cloudscape local --exposure 0` for the earlier cloud and exposure treatment; both modes now share the larger terrain extent. It supports Blender's newer `SINGLE_SCATTERING` name and older `NISHITA` name.

`--exposure` adjusts AgX display exposure in stops, from −4 to +4, with defaults of +1.5 for shared air and 0 for the earlier sky mode. It changes the display treatment of the complete scene; it does not multiply physical extinction or add a fill light. Lighting remains scene-relative rather than calibrated photometry.

## VFX references

The layered approach takes inspiration from Framestore's [*The Aeronauts* breakdown](https://beforesandafters.com/2019/12/26/cloudscaping/), which describes photographed panoramas alongside sculpted volumetric clouds, and DNEG's [*Masters of the Air* interview](https://beforesandafters.com/2024/04/17/we-asked-ourselves-at-the-beginning-do-we-go-for-something-that-feels-right-or-that-is-right/), which discusses reference-driven cloud shapes and formations. Guerrilla's [Nubis³ presentation](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-cubed) is a useful reference for clouds seen both at a distance and during flight. This experiment adopts the principles of atmospheric depth, multiple distances, and readable silhouettes; its bounded forecast-derived atlases and shader treatment do not reproduce those productions' simulation systems.

## Current limits

- Weather is frozen at the shot's midpoint. Aircraft/camera motion gives parallax through that field, but this experiment does not animate its meteorology or wind over the shot.
- The maneuver follows a local tangent plane. Its MSL altitude rises slightly as it travels away from the origin; it is not an exact recorded flight replay.
- Terrain is a coarse curved patch with historical seasonal imagery. It is not surveyed near-airport scenery or a detailed ocean simulation.
- The aircraft is a dimensional illustration. City emission, Moon lighting, labels, telemetry, motion blur and a globe-to-chase transition are not included in this separate experiment.
- Prepared assets are cached by trip, settings, source and bundle content. Frames are rendered anew on each invocation; this command does not offer resumable rendering. The selected backend and render settings are deliberately part of this single cache key, isolating their jobs and outputs; retuning resolution, samples or cloud detail therefore rebakes the local assets. Sharing prepared assets across render configurations is a future optimization. Blender and device versions are recorded in the render report rather than the preparation key. GPU noise and performance can vary with the runtime.

Inspect cloud shape, horizon stability, aircraft visibility, parallax and exposure in the actual movie before promoting a camera treatment to production. The most useful next steps are animated volume samples, more efficient atmospheric sampling, richer close-range cloud detail, supplied-track following, and a continuous globe-to-chase transition. All are intended to run locally; this camera adds no hosted service dependency.

## Output and checks

The work directory contains `job.json` (inputs, clocks, poses, limits and source hashes), `terrain.npz`, optional `cloud.npz`/`cloud.json` and `cloud-far.npz`/`cloud-far.json`, `device.json`, `render-report.json`, PNG frames and, after a full render, `chase.mp4` plus `verification.json`. Bundle paths in the job are local references, so share the movie and a sanitized provenance summary rather than publishing the work directory.

Portable tests cover the camera basis, screen centering, level horizon, coordinated-turn rate, frame-order independence, ENU/ECEF conversion, physical cloud-height mapping, float atlas storage, complementary near/far weights, bounded silhouette warping, clear-column preservation, native grid bounds and discretization, atmospheric extinction references, UTC validation, dimensions, material/terrain calibration inputs and relocated verified asset bundles. A real Blender render is still needed to verify the shader and visual result.
