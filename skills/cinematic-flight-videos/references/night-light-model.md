# Regional city-light model

City lighting is added as scene-relative linear RGB at the Earth surface, before atmospheric and cloud attenuation. Directly sunlit diffuse white is approximately 1 in this shared scale. The brightest source has a modeled emitted luminance of 0.45 before population activation and `style.city_light_strength`. Exposure and tone mapping happen once after scene composition. Neither the source texture nor this scale is calibrated radiance.

The production model separates historical source locations, regional fixture mixtures, causal photocell state and local occupancy schedules. A geographic source pixel with zero emission stays zero, including open ocean. Texture intensity never assigns a fixture type, a switch threshold or a color temperature.

## Source and uncertainty

The grayscale 2016 Black Marble display texture locates aggregate historical light. It is not live travel-date lighting and does not identify individual fixtures, land use, spectra or switch-on times. VIIRS DNB is panchromatic; this texture cannot provide measured correlated color temperatures. NASA's scientific nighttime products correct several observational effects and combine observations; the supplied display image is not those calibrated measurements. [NASA nighttime-light product description](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/science-domain/nighttime-lights)

The model uses overlapping spherical regional priors for North America, Europe, East Asia, South Asia, Africa, South America and Oceania, plus a substantial default prior everywhere. Their lamp contributions, curfews and geographic widths are illustrative choices, not surveyed regional averages or administrative boundaries. Six representative CIE spectra supply sodium, four white-LED families and metal-halide emission colors. These are converted to linear sRGB and normalized to equal photopic luminance; see [reference spectra, conversion and licensing](lamp-spectra.md). Family names use nominal color temperatures: the `led_4000` reference, for example, is approximately 4103 K. The renderer remains RGB and does not trace individual wavelengths.

Outdoor, building, sign and persistent populations each have their own lamp mixture. Their contributions are summed as population RGB multiplied by population activity, so source chromaticity can change as different lights activate or dim. Every population color has unit luminance, preserving the previous scalar emission budget at matched shares and activity. Mixture weights represent luminous contributions, not fixture counts. Broad regional priors and stable 18 km district variation alter these mixtures independently of source texture brightness. Spherical coordinates maintain continuity at the dateline and poles; no randomness depends on the camera or frame order.

Optional version-2 [local profiles](local-assets.md) override selected populations within explicit geography and physical dates. Omitted populations retain the default model. The Chicago/Auckland example anchors outdoor colors to published municipal lighting programs, while identifying its luminous proportions, circular coverage and validity window as modeled. It is never enabled automatically. Version-1 `xy` profiles remain supported and apply their common chromaticity to every population. Historical imagery still determines where emission exists; profiles cannot invent lights in zero-source pixels.

The same source RGB illuminates the ground image and cloud volumes. Cloud and air transport can blend and attenuate that color. The final per-channel Reinhard shoulder naturally reduces saturation at high exposures; it is unchanged in this release. Comparisons should hold brightness, exposure, camera and weather fixed before attributing a visible change to lamp technology. Reference spectra constrain plausible source colors, not measured city-wide color temperature or absolute radiance.

## Causal photocontrols

Clear-sky horizontal illuminance is interpolated in log lux. Solar elevations 0°, −3°, −6° and −9° use approximately 430, 43, 1.35 and 0.054 lux, respectively. These rounded anchors come from Hulburt's observations at Sacramento Peak, 2,800 m elevation. They are representative anchors, not a global sensor calibration; other daytime and nighttime anchors are assumed extensions. [Hulburt, 1952](https://opg.optica.org/josa/abstract.cfm?uri=josa-42-5-353)

Outdoor photocontrols use nominal 16.1-lux turn-on and 48.3-lux turn-off thresholds. These reflect the 1.5-foot-candle on and 4.5-foot-candle off manufacturer specification used by the earlier model. Geographic threshold variation and the unresolved population distribution are modeled. [Manufacturer specifications](https://www.intermatic.com/Catalog/us/Products/Photocontrols/Fixed-Mount-Electronic-Photocontrols/EK4136S)

Each control population contains 32 fixed threshold quantiles. Relays turn on below their lower threshold, turn off above their upper threshold and retain their previous state between thresholds. Dawn therefore differs from dusk because of actual prior state. A second, broader population represents earlier building and sign activation around a modeled 100-lux threshold, with a modeled 1.8-times off/on ratio. The building threshold is not a building-code requirement or measured global mean.

Weather can reduce the photocells' estimated ambient sky illumination. The estimate combines forecast cloud cover and optical mass with a bounded diffuse-transmission approximation, retaining at least eight percent of clear-sky illuminance. It does not reuse direct-Sun shadow as though that were a sensor's hemispheric illuminance. It is an approximate weather response, not observed lux. Clouds can trigger relays and the deadband can preserve the resulting activation after illuminance recovers. The separate camera-ray attenuation still dims emitted light on its path to the viewer.

## Schedules and clocks

Four emission populations represent outdoor lights, occupied buildings, signs and persistent sources. Their modeled shares sum to one. Outdoor sources have modest overnight dimming; building and sign schedules peak during the evening and reduce after regional curfews. A morning building contribution represents early occupancy. Persistent sources remain active through day and night. These fractions are neither occupancy forecasts nor recovered satellite switching schedules.

Schedules use **local mean solar time**, calculated from physical UTC and longitude. This is deliberately not an authoritative civil-time-zone map: no political time-zone boundaries, DST changes, holidays or jurisdiction-specific curfews are inferred. An airport's time zone is not extrapolated over neighboring regions. Schedules are periodic across midnight and continuous at the dateline. Near either pole they tend to a longitude-independent daily average because longitude, and hence local solar hour, becomes undefined at the pole.

Physical UTC drives the Sun, relay history and schedules. Weather UTC drives only meteorology; illustrative replay remaps weather without moving the Sun or occupancy clock. When the physical clock is held at departure or arrival, lamp state is held too, even if presentation clouds keep moving. Pre-trip initialization weather clamps to available fields and is identified in the cache metadata.

## Preparation and sampling

`prepare_city_lights(trip, work, physical_times=...)` runs before rendering workers. The CLI supplies all exact frame UTCs. The solver advances chronologically with internal steps no longer than five physical minutes and a 24-hour warmup before the first requested time. At each requested instant it records two aggregate relay fractions on a default two-degree global grid. Frame-order-independent workers read immutable memory-mapped arrays and interpolate spatially. Unprepared intermediate instants use the previous prepared state; they never interpolate a future activation backward in time.

Snapshots are retained only for requested times. Long unobserved gaps over 48 hours restart from a new 24-hour warmup, rather than allocating or integrating every day of a decade-long layover. The requested samples are still evaluated at their true calendar dates. State storage is capped at 256 MiB by coarsening the global grid when necessary. Effective grid size, sample count, integration steps, warmup, gap restarts and modeling limitations are written to `city-lights/metadata.json`.

The initial state during persistent polar twilight cannot be recovered from a historical light composite. It uses an explicit unobserved threshold prior; a 24-hour warmup cannot guarantee a previous daylight/night crossing near the poles. Cloud changes shorter than the integration interval and hardware delays lasting seconds are unresolved. The renderer does not stretch a seconds-long relay delay into a cinematic fade. Thirty-two unresolved quantiles and spatial sampling provide aggregate transitions; exact frame snapshots avoid a five-minute hold during ordinary playback.

## Validation

The portable tests check genuine dawn/dusk hysteresis, cloud-triggered persistence, zero-source ocean pixels, normalized regional mixtures, unclipped linear output, persistent daytime emission, midnight continuity, the explicit no-DST clock policy, dateline and pole continuity, multi-day layovers, bounded decade-gap preparation, immutable memory maps, shuffled frame order and repeated visits. The earlier instantaneous diagnostic functions remain available in `citylight_model.py`; production radiance does not use their separate display-visibility gain.
